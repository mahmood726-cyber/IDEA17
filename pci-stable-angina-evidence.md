# Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) for Stable Angina
## A Catastrophic NNT/NNH Reversal in Modern Medicine

---

## Executive Summary

**The Verdict**: PCI for stable angina represents the most commercially widespread and clinically devastating example of an intervention rendered obsolete by medical therapy. With an **NNT of infinity** for both prognostic and symptomatic benefit, combined with an **NNH of 20-50** for peri-procedural harm, this multi-billion dollar industry was built on a flawed causal model that collapsed when optimal medical therapy severed the link between stenosis and outcomes.

---

## 🎯 The Target: The "Plaque-Busting" Doctrine

### The Foundation (1990s-2000s)

The entire field of interventional cardiology was built on a deceptively intuitive premise:

**The "Oculo-Stenotic Reflex"**: *See a blockage, fix a blockage*

**Core Assumptions:**
- Significant coronary stenosis (>70%) = "ticking time bomb" for heart attack
- Stenting = life-saving, prognostic intervention
- The degree of stenosis was a direct proxy for risk of myocardial infarction (MI)

### The Original Evidence Base

**RITA-2 (1999)** and similar early trials:
- Compared PCI to "medical therapy" for stable symptoms
- **Finding**: PCI superior for symptom control
- **Critical Flaw**: Medical therapy in 1999 was primitive (aspirin, beta-blocker, maybe a long-acting nitrate)
- **Assumption**: Symptomatic benefit implied prognostic benefit

### The Resulting Doctrine

**Clinical Guidelines Built Around:**
1. Revascularization to prevent future cardiac events
2. Symptom relief as secondary benefit
3. Aggressive intervention for any significant stenosis

**Market Impact**: Multi-billion dollar industry, millions of procedures annually

---

## ⚠️ The Baseline Harm (NNH)

### Peri-procedural Risks

**Combined NNH**: **20-50** (2-5% risk of major complications)

**Major Complications Include:**
- Death
- Stroke
- Emergency coronary artery bypass surgery (CABG)
- Major bleeding
- Vascular complications
- Contrast-induced nephropathy

**Critical Point**: This harm is immediate, measurable, and unavoidable. Every patient accepts this risk up-front.

---

## 🔥 The Revolution: Optimal Medical Therapy (OMT)

### The Paradigm Shift

The "medical therapy" of the 1990s was **symptomatic treatment only**:
- Aspirin
- Beta-blocker
- Long-acting nitrate

### The Discovery: High-Intensity Statins

**The Game-Changer**: High-dose statins were not just cholesterol-lowering drugs. They are the **most powerful plaque-stabilizing agents in existence**.

**Mechanism of Action:**
1. "Cools off" inflamed coronary plaques
2. Thickens the fibrous cap
3. Dramatically reduces rupture risk (the true cause of MI)

**The Carotid Parallel**: Just like in carotid artery disease, statins transformed the pathophysiology by targeting plaque *stability*, not stenosis *severity*.

### The Causal Model Failure

**The 1990s Model (WRONG)**:
```
Stenosis % → Flow Limitation → MI Risk
```

**The Actual Pathophysiology**:
```
Plaque Instability → Rupture → Thrombosis → MI
```

**Critical Insight**: Stenosis severity and plaque vulnerability are **poorly correlated**. A 50% stenosis with an inflamed, vulnerable cap is more dangerous than a stable 80% stenosis.

### Modern Optimal Medical Therapy (OMT)

**Components:**
1. High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80mg or rosuvastatin 40mg)
2. ACE inhibitor or ARB
3. Aspirin (antiplatelet)
4. Beta-blocker (for angina control)
5. Lifestyle modification

**Result**: Transformed stable angina from a "pre-MI" state into a **benign, chronic inflammatory condition** with excellent prognosis.

**Baseline Risk Collapse**: The annual MI risk in the OMT control group plummeted from 5-8% (1990s) to <1% (modern era).

## 💥 The Proof: Three Landmark Trials

### 1. COURAGE Trial (2007) – The Prognostic Failure

**Design**: The "DANISH trial" equivalent for stents
- **N**: 2,287 patients with stable angina and significant stenosis
- **Arms**:
  - PCI + OMT
  - OMT Alone
- **Follow-up**: 5 years

**Primary Endpoint**: Death or myocardial infarction

**Results**:
| Outcome | PCI + OMT | OMT Alone | p-value |
|---------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Death or MI | 19.0% | 18.5% | 0.62 (NS) |

**The Devastating Finding**: **Absolutely no difference** in death or MI over 5 years.

**Analysis**:
- **NNT for prognostic benefit**: **INFINITE**
- The OMT-alone baseline risk was so low (thanks to statins) that stenting added **nothing but cost and risk**
- The entire prognostic justification for the procedure evaporated

**Citation**: Boden WE, et al. N Engl J Med. 2007;356(15):1503-1516.

---

### 2. ORBITA Trial (2018) – The Symptomatic Failure

**Design**: The ultimate methodological critique - **sham-controlled RCT**

**Background**: After COURAGE, proponents retreated to "symptom control" as the last remaining justification. ORBITA tested this with scientific rigor.

**Method**:
- **N**: 200 patients with stable angina and ≥70% stenosis
- **Arms**:
  - PCI + OMT
  - **Sham procedure** + OMT (catheter inserted, no stent placed)
- **Blinding**: Patients, physicians, and outcome assessors all blinded
- **Primary endpoint**: Exercise time on treadmill

**Results**:

| Outcome | PCI + OMT | Sham + OMT | Difference | p-value |
|---------|-----------|------------|------------|---------|
| Exercise time increase | +28.4 sec | +11.8 sec | +16.6 sec | 0.20 (NS) |
| Angina frequency | Improved | Improved | No difference | NS |

**The Devastating Finding**: PCI was **no better than placebo** at improving angina symptoms or exercise capacity.

**Analysis**:
- **NNT for symptom benefit**: **INFINITE**
- The entire "benefit" seen for 40 years was a combination of:
  1. **Placebo effect** (powerful in subjective endpoints like chest pain)
  2. **Adjunctive OMT** (patients in both groups got better because of the drugs)
- The stent itself contributed **zero** benefit

**Citation**: Al-Lamee R, et al. Lancet. 2018;391(10115):31-40.

---

### 3. ISCHEMIA Trial (2020) – The Final Confirmation

**Design**: Large-scale international trial
- **N**: 5,179 patients with moderate-to-severe ischemia
- **Arms**:
  - Initial invasive strategy (PCI or CABG) + OMT
  - Conservative strategy (OMT alone)
- **Follow-up**: Median 3.2 years

**Primary Endpoint**: Composite of cardiovascular death, MI, or hospitalization for unstable angina

**Results**:
| Outcome | Invasive + OMT | Conservative OMT | HR (95% CI) |
|---------|----------------|------------------|-------------|
| Primary endpoint | 13.3% | 15.5% | 0.93 (0.80-1.08) |
| Death or MI | 11.7% | 13.9% | 0.95 (0.80-1.12) |

**The Finding**: **No significant difference** in death, MI, or major cardiovascular events.

**Quality of Life**: Small early advantage for invasive strategy in angina frequency, but **no difference by 3 years**.

**Analysis**:
- Confirmed COURAGE findings in a larger, more diverse population
- Even in patients with *moderate-to-severe ischemia*, OMT alone was non-inferior
- The "stent saves lives" narrative definitively disproven

**Citation**: Maron DJ, et al. N Engl J Med. 2020;382(15):1395-1407.

**Current Practice**: Millions of procedures annually with:
- **NNT = ∞** (no measurable benefit)

## 🔬 Why the Old Meta-Analyses Are Fundamentally Wrong

### The Fatal Flaw: Historical Control Bias

**Problem**: Meta-analyses including pre-2000 trials are comparing:
- **PCI + OMT** (modern)
- vs. **Symptomatic therapy alone** (aspirin + beta-blocker)

**This is NOT a comparison of PCI vs. no-PCI**. It's a comparison of **OMT vs. no-OMT**, with PCI as a confounding variable.

### The Causal Severance

**The 1990s Causal Chain**:
```
Stenosis → Ischemia → Plaque disruption → MI
```
**Therefore**: Remove stenosis → Prevent MI ✗ (WRONG)

**The Actual Causal Chain**:
```
Inflammation → Vulnerable plaque → Rupture → Thrombosis → MI
```
**Key Insight**: Stenosis severity ≠ Plaque vulnerability

**What Statins Do**: Attack the *actual* causal mechanism
```
Statins → ↓ Inflammation → Stable plaque → No rupture → No MI ✓
```

**Result**: Statins severed the link between stenosis and outcomes. The old model became obsolete.

---

## 🎓 The Methodological Lessons

### 1. The Surrogate Endpoint Trap

**Stenosis %** was treated as a validated surrogate for MI risk. It was not.

**Lesson**: Surrogate endpoints must be validated against hard outcomes, not assumed.

### 2. The Importance of Sham Controls

**Before ORBITA**: 40 years of "obvious" symptomatic benefit
**After ORBITA**: 100% of the benefit was placebo + OMT

**Lesson**: Subjective endpoints (chest pain) *require* sham controls to separate procedure from placebo.

### 3. The "Mechanism Matters" Principle

**The Error**: Assumed the mechanism of MI was flow limitation from stenosis
**The Reality**: Mechanism is plaque rupture, largely independent of stenosis severity

**Lesson**: Treating the wrong mechanism, even effectively, produces no benefit.

### 4. The Drug-Induced Evidence Decay

**Novel Insight**: OMT didn't just *add* benefit. It *eliminated* the disease mechanism that PCI targeted.

**Result**: Old evidence became not just dated, but **fundamentally inapplicable**.

## 📋 Current Guidelines (Post-ISCHEMIA)

### Major Society Recommendations

**American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (2021)**:
- **Class I**: OMT for all patients with stable ischemic heart disease
- **Class IIa**: PCI may be reasonable for *refractory angina* despite OMT
- **Critical**: No longer recommended for prognostic benefit in stable disease

**European Society of Cardiology (2019, updated 2020)**:
- OMT as first-line therapy
- PCI only after OMT failure for symptom control
- Explicit statement: PCI does not reduce mortality in stable CAD

### The Retreat

**What Changed**: Guidelines shifted from:
- "PCI to prevent MI and improve symptoms"

To:
- "OMT for all; PCI only for refractory symptoms despite maximal medical therapy"

**Remaining Controversy**: Even the symptomatic indication is questionable post-ORBITA.

## 💡 The "Common Sense" Trap

### Why This Persists

**Intuitive Appeal**: "If there's a blockage, shouldn't we unblock it?"

**Visual Bias**: Angiograms showing severe stenosis are visually compelling

**Immediate Gratification**: Patients feel better immediately (placebo + procedure ritual)

**Financial Incentives**: Massive industry with entrenched interests

**Cognitive Dissonance**: Difficult for interventional cardiologists to accept that their life's work may not provide benefit

### The Counter-Intuitive Truth

**Reality**: The heart is resilient.
- Collateral circulation develops around chronic blockages
- Stable plaques are not "about to cause a heart attack"
- The degree of stenosis visible on angiography is a poor predictor of future events
- The *real* danger is invisible: thin-capped, inflamed plaques that may cause only 30-40% stenosis

**The Statin Miracle**: Stabilizes the vulnerable plaque, regardless of stenosis degree

## 🔮 Future Directions

### What Should Replace PCI?

**For Stable Angina:**
1. **Aggressive OMT**: High-dose statin, antiplatelet, ACE-I, beta-blocker
2. **Lifestyle modification**: Exercise, diet, smoking cessation
3. **Symptom management**: Titrate anti-anginal medications
4. **Watchful waiting**: Most patients improve on OMT alone

**Reserved for**:
- **Acute coronary syndromes** (STEMI, NSTEMI) - where PCI *is* life-saving
- **True refractory angina** limiting quality of life despite maximal OMT
- **Left main or severe three-vessel disease** with poor LV function

### Ongoing Research

**Emerging Questions**:
1. Can advanced imaging (FFR-CT, OCT) identify the subset who truly benefit?
2. What is the real placebo-adjusted NNT for symptom relief in OMT-refractory patients?
3. How do we de-implement a practice embedded in practice patterns and economics?
