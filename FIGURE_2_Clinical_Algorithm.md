---

## DO NOT PERFORM PCI FOR:

❌ **Prognostic benefit** (preventing death/MI) in stable angina without high-risk anatomy
- Evidence: COURAGE, ISCHEMIA (Class III - No Benefit)

❌ **Asymptomatic patients** with angiographic stenosis
- Stenosis severity ≠ event risk
- FFR >0.80: Excellent outcomes on OMT alone

❌ **Well-controlled symptoms** on medical therapy
- Evidence: ORBITA showed no benefit over placebo when on OMT

---

## SPECIFIC SCENARIOS:

### Scenario 1: Asymptomatic 65yo with 70% LAD stenosis
**Recommendation**: OMT only
**Rationale**: No symptoms, no high-risk anatomy → No indication for PCI
**Evidence**: COURAGE, ISCHEMIA showed no prognostic benefit

### Scenario 2: 58yo with angina improved on OMT (1-2 episodes/week, not limiting)
**Recommendation**: Continue OMT, reinforce adherence
**Rationale**: Symptoms controlled → PCI unlikely to add benefit
**Evidence**: ORBITA showed no benefit when on medications

### Scenario 3: 62yo with severe angina despite maximal OMT (daily symptoms limiting activity)
**Recommendation**: Consider FFR-guided PCI (Class IIa)
**Rationale**: Refractory symptoms → May benefit from PCI
**Evidence**: ORBITA-2 showed benefit when symptoms persist; FAME-2 reduced urgent revascularization
**Critical**: Discuss that PCI won't prevent MI/death, only relieve symptoms

### Scenario 4: 70yo with 80% left main stenosis
**Recommendation**: Revascularization (PCI or CABG based on anatomy/SYNTAX score)
**Rationale**: High-risk anatomy → Prognostic indication
**Evidence**: Excluded from COURAGE/ISCHEMIA; observational data supports revascularization

### Scenario 5: 55yo with 60% stenosis, FFR 0.85
**Recommendation**: OMT only
**Rationale**: No hemodynamic significance by FFR
**Evidence**: FAME-2 registry - FFR >0.80 had excellent outcomes on OMT alone

---

## KEY MESSAGES FOR CLINICIANS:

1. **START WITH OMT FOR ALL PATIENTS**
   - Foundation of treatment
   - Provides excellent prognostic outcomes
   - High-intensity statin is critical

2. **PCI IS FOR SYMPTOMS, NOT SURVIVAL** (in stable angina)
   - Does not prevent death or MI
   - May relieve refractory angina
   - Requires informed consent

3. **USE PHYSIOLOGY, NOT JUST ANATOMY**
   - FFR/iFR helps identify hemodynamically significant lesions
   - Angiographic stenosis alone is insufficient indication

4. **HIGH-RISK ANATOMY IS DIFFERENT**
   - Left main, proximal LAD, multivessel + ↓LVEF
   - These may warrant revascularization for prognosis
   - Excluded from major trials

5. **MEDICATION STATUS MATTERS**
   - PCI provides symptomatic benefit when OFF antianginals (ORBITA-2)
   - No benefit when ON optimized therapy (ORBITA)
   - Consider optimizing medical therapy FIRST

**Figure 2 Legend**: Evidence-based clinical decision algorithm for management of stable angina. All patients should receive optimal medical therapy (OMT) as first-line treatment. PCI should be reserved for patients with high-risk anatomy (prognostic indication) or refractory symptoms despite maximal medical therapy (symptomatic indication). Routine PCI for asymptomatic patients or those with well-controlled symptoms is not supported by evidence. FFR = fractional flow reserve; iFR = instantaneous wave-free ratio; LAD = left anterior descending artery; LVEF = left ventricular ejection fraction; REVASC = revascularization; DAPT = dual antiplatelet therapy; Class IIa/III = ACC/AHA guideline recommendation classes.
