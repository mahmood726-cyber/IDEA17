## Overview

This document summarizes the comprehensive revisions made to address all concerns identified in the editorial peer review. The document has been transformed from a polemical advocacy piece to a balanced, scientifically rigorous evidence synthesis.

**Result**: Document now suitable for academic publication with balanced presentation of evidence.

## Major Concerns Addressed (All 8 Required Changes Completed)

### ✅ 1. CRITICAL: Added ORBITA-2 Trial (2023)

**Editorial Concern**: Fatal flaw - document omitted ORBITA-2 which showed OPPOSITE results to ORBITA regarding symptomatic benefit.

**Changes Made**:
- Added complete ORBITA-2 trial section (lines 178-217)
- Included full results: Angina score 2.9 vs 5.6 (OR 2.21, p<0.001)
- Documented 3x higher likelihood of angina freedom with PCI
- Explained key difference: patients OFF antianginal medications
- Updated all conclusions to reflect context-dependent symptomatic benefit
- Added to summary table showing when PCI does/doesn't provide symptomatic benefit

**Impact**: Completely revised claims about symptomatic benefit from "NNT = infinity" to "context-dependent; benefit demonstrated when patients not on antianginal medications"

---

### ✅ 2. CRITICAL: Added FAME-2 Trial (2018)

**Editorial Concern**: Missing critical evidence showing benefit for FFR-guided (vs angiography-guided) PCI.

**Changes Made**:
- Added complete FAME-2 trial section (lines 250-294)
- Included 5-year results: 13.9% vs 27.0% composite endpoint (HR 0.46, p<0.001)
- Documented benefit driven by reduction in urgent revascularization
- Explained FFR >0.80 patients did well on OMT alone
- Contrasted physiologic vs anatomic assessment approaches
- Integrated into overall evidence synthesis

**Impact**: Document now acknowledges that FFR-guided patient selection may identify those who benefit from PCI, rather than claiming universal lack of benefit.

---

### ✅ 3. CRITICAL: Fixed "NNT = ∞" Misuse

**Editorial Concern**: Mathematically and statistically incorrect use of "NNT = infinity" for non-significant results.

**Changes Made**:
- Removed ALL instances of "NNT = ∞" or "NNT = infinite"
- Replaced with scientifically accurate language:
  - "No statistically significant benefit demonstrated"
  - "Did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction"
  - "The difference was not statistically significant"
- Removed inflammatory "infinite NNT" rhetoric throughout
- Created evidence summary table instead (lines 299-326)

**Impact**: Document now uses proper statistical terminology acknowledging difference between "no proven benefit" and "proven no benefit."

---

### ✅ 4. CRITICAL: Tone Adjusted Throughout

**Editorial Concern**: Excessively polemical tone with emojis and advocacy language inappropriate for scientific discourse.

**Changes Made**:
- Removed ALL emojis from section headers (🎯, 🔥, 💥, 📊, 🔬, 🎓, 💡, 🔮, 📚)
- Changed dramatic headers:
  - "The Devastating Recalculation" → "Summary of Trial Evidence"
  - "The Prognostic Failure" → "PCI Plus OMT vs OMT Alone"
  - "The Symptomatic Failure" → "Sham-Controlled Trial"
- Removed superlatives: "catastrophic," "devastating," "fatal flaw," "evaporated"
- Eliminated phrases like "The Numbers Don't Lie"
- Changed subtitle from "A Catastrophic NNT/NNH Reversal" to "An Evidence-Based Analysis"
- Rewrote executive summary in neutral, scientific tone

**Impact**: Professional, academic tone suitable for peer-reviewed publication.

---

### ✅ 5. CRITICAL: Fixed NNH Calculations with Proper Citations

**Editorial Concern**: Claimed "NNH = 20-50" without specific citations; likely overstated harm.

**Changes Made**:
- Created detailed procedural risk table (lines 334-341) with specific complication rates
- Provided specific sources: COURAGE, ISCHEMIA, ACC-NCDR, contemporary registries
- Broke down by complication type:
  - Death: 0.2-0.5%
  - Stroke: 0.2-0.3%
  - Emergency CABG: 0.3-0.5%
  - Major bleeding: 1-2%
  - Acute kidney injury: 1-3%
- Calculated composite major complications: 2-3% → **NNH = 33-50** (not 20-50)
- Added context about modern techniques reducing complications
- Included specific references (Boden 2007, Maron 2020, Rao 2016)

**Impact**: Accurate, evidence-based harm assessment with proper attribution.

---

### ✅ 6. CRITICAL: Added Patient Selection and Subgroup Discussion

**Editorial Concern**: Treated all "stable angina" as homogeneous; failed to acknowledge subgroups that may benefit.

**Changes Made**:
- Created comprehensive summary table (lines 301-307) stratifying by trial, population, and benefit
- Added "Clinical Approach to Stable Angina" section (lines 541-561) specifying:
  - When to use OMT alone (first-line for all)
  - When to consider PCI (refractory angina, FFR+, intolerance to meds, high-risk anatomy)
  - When NOT to use PCI (routine prognostic benefit, asymptomatic, controlled symptoms)
- Specified anatomic high-risk features: left main, proximal LAD, multivessel + reduced LVEF
- Integrated FFR/physiologic assessment throughout
- Added "Patient Selection Matters" section (lines 565-568)

**Impact**: Nuanced, individualized approach rather than blanket statements.

---

### ✅ 7. CRITICAL: Added Comprehensive Limitations Section

**Editorial Concern**: No discussion of trial limitations, remaining uncertainties, or populations not studied.

**Changes Made**:
- Created full "Limitations and Areas of Uncertainty" section (lines 423-488) including:
  - **Trial-specific limitations** for COURAGE, ORBITA, ORBITA-2, ISCHEMIA, FAME-2
  - **7 remaining clinical questions** (timing, long-term benefit, FFR threshold, etc.)
  - **Populations not well-studied** (left main, elderly, CKD, prior CABG, CTO, microvascular)
  - **Technological evolution** not captured in trials (newer stents, IVUS/OCT, iFR, radial access)
- Acknowledged power issues, exclusion criteria, crossover, early stopping

**Impact**: Transparent about evidence gaps and limitations rather than overstating certainty.

---

### ✅ 8. CRITICAL: Completely Rewrote Conclusions

**Editorial Concern**: Conclusions were overly absolute and failed to acknowledge appropriate use cases.

**Changes Made**:
- Replaced "give statins, not stents" with comprehensive evidence-based recommendations (lines 519-609)
- New conclusion structure:
  - Evidence-based conclusions (prognostic, symptomatic, physiologic assessment)
  - Clinical approach algorithm
  - Key insights from evidence
  - Balanced perspective
  - What evidence DOES vs DOES NOT support
- Changed from: "NNT = ∞, NNH = 20-50, give statins not stents"
- Changed to: "Start with OMT for all. Reserve PCI for selected patients with refractory symptoms, FFR+ lesions, or high-risk anatomy. Patient selection and shared decision-making essential."

**Impact**: Clinically useful, evidence-based guidance rather than absolutist rhetoric.
