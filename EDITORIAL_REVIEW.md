# EDITORIAL REVIEW: PCI for Stable Angina Evidence Analysis

**Reviewer**: Journal Editor Review

## OVERALL ASSESSMENT

This manuscript presents an important critique of PCI for stable angina, drawing on landmark trials (COURAGE, ORBITA, ISCHEMIA). However, **the document has significant methodological and interpretative flaws that undermine its credibility**. While the core thesis has merit, the presentation is unbalanced, omits critical evidence, misuses statistical terminology, and adopts a polemical tone inappropriate for scientific discourse.

**DECISION**: Major revisions required before this can be considered a rigorous evidence-based analysis.

---

## MAJOR CONCERNS (Must Be Addressed)

### 1. CRITICAL OMISSION: ORBITA-2 Trial (2023)

**Severity**: ⚠️ **FATAL FLAW**

**Issue**: The document bases its claim that "NNT for symptom benefit = INFINITE" entirely on ORBITA (2018), but **completely omits ORBITA-2** (published NEJM, November 2023), which showed **OPPOSITE results**.

**ORBITA-2 Results:**
- N = 301 patients with stable angina
- **Primary endpoint**: Angina symptom score: PCI 2.9 vs placebo 5.6 (OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.41-3.47, **p < 0.001**)
- **Finding**: PCI provided **significant symptomatic benefit** vs sham procedure
- Patients were 3x more likely to be angina-free with PCI vs placebo

**Why This Matters**:
- ORBITA-2 directly contradicts the document's central claim about symptomatic benefit
- The key difference: ORBITA-2 patients were NOT on antianginal medications, whereas ORBITA patients were on OMT
- This suggests PCI provides symptomatic benefit in patients with ongoing angina despite OMT withdrawal

**Required Action**:
- Include and discuss ORBITA-2
- Revise the "NNT for symptoms = infinity" claim
- Acknowledge that symptomatic benefit may exist in specific patient populations
- The correct interpretation: PCI may provide symptomatic benefit when antianginal medications are not used or have failed

**Citation**: Al-Lamee R, et al. N Engl J Med. 2024;390(2):115-125.

### 2. CRITICAL OMISSION: FAME-2 Trial

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document presents only angiography-guided PCI trials but omits **FAME-2**, which showed benefit for **FFR-guided PCI** over medical therapy alone.

**FAME-2 Results (5-year follow-up):**
- N = 888 patients with FFR ≤0.80 (hemodynamically significant stenosis)
- **Primary endpoint**: Death, MI, or urgent revascularization
  - PCI + OMT: 13.9%
  - OMT alone: 27.0%
  - HR 0.46 (95% CI 0.34-0.63), **p < 0.001**
- **Finding**: FFR-guided PCI reduced the composite endpoint by 54%

**Why This Matters**:
- Demonstrates that physiologically-significant stenosis (not just angiographic) may benefit from PCI
- Suggests that patient selection matters - not all "stable angina" is the same
- The "NNT = infinity" claim is contradicted by this trial

**Important Nuance**:
- The benefit was driven primarily by urgent revascularization (not death/MI alone)
- But this still represents a meaningful clinical benefit
- MI rate numerically lower in PCI group (8.1% vs 12.0%), though not statistically significant

**Required Action**:
- Include FAME-2 in the evidence review
- Acknowledge that FFR-guided (vs. angiography-guided) PCI may have different outcomes
- Revise claims to specify "angiography-guided PCI" where appropriate
- Discuss implications: perhaps the issue is patient selection, not PCI itself

**Citation**: Fearon WF, et al. N Engl J Med. 2018;379(3):250-259.

### 3. MISUSE OF STATISTICAL TERMINOLOGY: "NNT = ∞"

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document repeatedly states "NNT = infinity" for interventions showing no statistically significant benefit. This is **mathematically and statistically incorrect**.

**The Problem**:
- When there is no significant difference between groups, the appropriate statement is: "**NNT cannot be calculated**" or "**No significant benefit was demonstrated**"
- An "infinite" NNT is meaningless - it implies you'd need to treat infinite patients to get one benefit
- What the data actually shows: The difference between groups is **not statistically distinguishable from zero**, which is NOT the same as "the benefit is exactly zero"

**Correct Interpretation**:

| Trial | Finding | CORRECT Statement | INCORRECT Statement |
|-------|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| COURAGE | HR 1.05, p=0.62 | No significant reduction in death/MI | NNT = ∞ |
| ORBITA | Diff +16.6s, p=0.20 | No significant improvement in exercise time | NNT = ∞ |

**Why This Matters**:
- "No proven benefit" ≠ "Proven no benefit"
- Non-significant results could reflect: (a) true lack of benefit, (b) underpowered studies, (c) small true benefit below detection threshold
- Using inflammatory language ("infinity") undermines scientific credibility

**Required Action**:
- Replace all instances of "NNT = ∞" with scientifically accurate language:
  - "No statistically significant benefit was demonstrated"
  - "NNT could not be calculated due to lack of significant difference"
  - "The observed difference was not statistically significant"

### 4. OVERSIMPLIFICATION: ORBITA Interpretation

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document states "PCI was no better than placebo" and "100% of the benefit was placebo + OMT" based on ORBITA, but this **overstates** what the trial actually showed.

**What ORBITA Actually Showed**:
- Primary endpoint: Exercise time increased +28.4s (PCI) vs +11.8s (sham)
- **Difference**: +16.6 seconds (95% CI: -8.9 to +42.0), **p = 0.20**
- This means: There was a TREND toward benefit that didn't reach statistical significance

**Critical Limitations of ORBITA**:
1. **Small sample size**: N = 200 (underpowered to detect modest benefit)
2. **Single-vessel disease only**: Not generalizable to multi-vessel CAD
3. **Short follow-up**: 6 weeks only
4. **Wide confidence interval**: Could represent anything from 9-second harm to 42-second benefit
5. **Enrollment bias**: Excluded many typical PCI candidates

**The Correct Interpretation**:
- ORBITA failed to demonstrate statistically significant symptomatic benefit
- This does NOT prove that PCI provides ZERO benefit
- It suggests that IF benefit exists, it's smaller than previously believed
- The trial was underpowered to definitively answer the question

**Required Action**:
- Acknowledge ORBITA's limitations (small size, single-vessel disease, short follow-up)
- Remove absolute statements like "contributed ZERO benefit"
- Use more nuanced language: "did not demonstrate significant benefit" rather than "provides no benefit"
- Discuss the confidence interval, not just the p-value

### 5. INCOMPLETE HARM ASSESSMENT (NNH Calculation)

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document claims "NNH = 20-50" based on "meta-analyses, registry data" but provides **no specific citations** and doesn't specify which complications are included.

**Problems**:
1. **No primary source cited**: Which meta-analysis? Which registry?
2. **Unclear definition**: What events count toward the 2-5% rate?
   - Death only?
   - Death + stroke + MI?
   - Death + stroke + MI + major bleeding + vascular complications?
3. **Population mismatch**: Is this NNH from stable angina PCI or from all PCI (including ACS)?
4. **Temporal validity**: Modern PCI techniques (radial access, newer stents) have lower complication rates than historical data

**What The Evidence Actually Shows** (from my search):
- **Modern PCI for stable CAD**: Complication rates are LOWER than 2-5%
- **CTO PCI** (highest risk): Mortality ~0.5-1.4%, urgent CABG ~0.5-4.0%
- **Standard PCI**: Death <0.5%, stroke <0.5%, major bleeding ~1-2%

**The Reality**: For routine stable angina PCI with modern techniques:
- Major complications (death, stroke, urgent CABG): ~1-2%
- This would give **NNH = 50-100**, not 20-50

**Required Action**:
- Provide specific citations for the NNH calculation
- Define explicitly which complications are included
- Use data specific to stable angina PCI (not all-comers PCI)
- Consider whether modern techniques have improved safety
- If using COURAGE/ISCHEMIA data, extract the actual procedural complication rates from those trials

### 6. MISSING NUANCE: Patient Selection and Subgroups

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document treats all "stable angina" as homogeneous, but **important subgroups may benefit** from PCI.

**Populations Likely Excluded or Underrepresented in COURAGE/ORBITA/ISCHEMIA**:
1. **Left main disease** (known to benefit from revascularization)
2. **Severe proximal LAD disease** (traditionally considered high-risk)
3. **Severe three-vessel disease with reduced LV function** (may benefit per STICH trial analogy)
4. **Hemodynamically significant stenosis by FFR** (FAME-2 showed benefit)
5. **Truly refractory angina** limiting quality of life (not just mild symptoms)

**Current Guidelines Acknowledge This**:
- ACC/AHA 2021: PCI "may be reasonable" for refractory angina (Class IIa)
- ESC 2019: PCI for symptom relief after OMT failure
- These guidelines POST-date COURAGE and ORBITA

**The Document's Claim**: "Millions of procedures annually with NNT = ∞"

**The Reality**:
- How many current PCIs are truly for "stable angina" vs. NSTEMI/ACS?
- Of stable angina PCIs, how many are for truly refractory symptoms?
- Guidelines have already changed practice patterns

**Required Action**:
- Acknowledge that not all stable angina is the same
- Discuss subgroups that may benefit (left main, proximal LAD, FFR+ lesions, refractory symptoms)
- Provide data on current PCI volumes: what % are stable angina vs ACS?
- Acknowledge that guidelines have already shifted practice

### 7. TONE: Inappropriate for Scientific Discourse

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MAJOR**

**Issue**: The document uses **advocacy language** rather than balanced scientific analysis.

**Examples of Problematic Language**:
- "catastrophic NNT/NNH reversal"
- "devastating finding"
- "fatal flaw"
- "the entire justification...evaporated"
- Extensive use of emojis (🎯, 🔥, 💥, 📊, etc.)
- Dramatic section headers ("The Revolution", "The Proof")

**Why This Matters**:
- Scientific writing should be dispassionate and balanced
- Advocacy language suggests bias and undermines credibility
- Readers (especially clinicians) will discount overly polemical arguments
- The evidence should speak for itself without dramatic embellishment

**Comparison**:
- **Current**: "This is the most catastrophic NNT/NNH flip in modern medicine"
- **Appropriate**: "These trials demonstrated no significant benefit for PCI compared to medical therapy in stable angina"

**Required Action**:
- Remove all emojis
- Replace superlative language with neutral descriptions
- Remove dramatic framing ("Revolution," "Devastating")
- Present evidence objectively and let readers draw conclusions
- Acknowledge uncertainty and limitations

### 8. INCOMPLETE CAUSAL MECHANISM DISCUSSION

**Severity**: ⚠️ **MODERATE**

**Issue**: The document presents a **simplified dichotomy** between "stenosis" and "plaque vulnerability" but the reality is more nuanced.

**Current Claim**:
- "The 1990s Model (WRONG): Stenosis % → Flow Limitation → MI Risk"
- "The Actual Pathophysiology: Plaque Instability → Rupture → Thrombosis → MI"

**The Reality Is More Complex**:
1. **Some MIs DO occur at sites of severe stenosis** - it's not either/or
2. **Flow-limiting stenosis CAN contribute to MI risk** through:
   - Supply-demand mismatch during stress
   - Downstream microinfarctions
   - Predisposition to thrombosis in low-flow states
3. **Plaque morphology and stenosis severity are PARTIALLY correlated**
4. **Not all vulnerable plaques rupture** - many heal silently

**What Statins Actually Do**:
- The document is correct that statins stabilize plaques
- But statins ALSO reduce atheroma volume and can regress stenosis
- And statins have multiple mechanisms (anti-inflammatory, anti-thrombotic, endothelial function)

**Required Action**:
- Acknowledge the mechanism is multifactorial
- Remove absolute "wrong/right" framing
- Present a more nuanced discussion:
  - Stenosis severity is an IMPERFECT predictor of MI risk
  - Plaque vulnerability is important but not the ONLY factor
  - Statins work through MULTIPLE mechanisms, not just plaque stabilization

## PROPOSED REVISED CONCLUSIONS

**Current Conclusion**:
> "For stable angina, give statins, not stents. NNT = ∞, NNH = 20-50."

**Revised Evidence-Based Conclusion**:

> **For most patients with stable angina and unobstructive or moderate coronary disease, optimal medical therapy should be the initial treatment strategy. Landmark trials (COURAGE, ISCHEMIA) have demonstrated that routine PCI does not reduce death or MI compared to OMT in this population.**
>
> **PCI may provide symptomatic benefit in selected patients with ongoing angina (ORBITA-2), particularly when antianginal medications are inadequate. FFR-guided PCI may benefit patients with hemodynamically significant stenosis (FAME-2).**
>
> **Populations likely to benefit from revascularization include: left main disease, severe proximal LAD stenosis, multivessel disease with reduced LV function, and truly refractory angina limiting quality of life despite maximal OMT.**
>
> **The key insight: Patient selection matters. Not all stenoses require intervention, and physiologic assessment (FFR, iFR) may identify those who benefit most.**
