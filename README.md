# PCI for Stable Angina — An Evidence-Based Analysis of Benefit and Harm

A critical synthesis of the evidence on percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)
in **stable** angina, arguing that routine PCI does not reduce death or myocardial
infarction versus optimal medical therapy (OMT), while OMT — particularly
high-intensity statins — has reshaped the natural history of stable coronary
disease. PCI's role is framed as symptom control in selected, physiology-guided
patients rather than a prognostic ("plaque-busting") intervention.

## Nature of this work

This is a **narrative evidence review and clinical synthesis**, not a registered
systematic review or meta-analysis. Claims are sourced to the major stable-angina
trials (e.g. COURAGE, ORBITA, ISCHEMIA) and guideline literature as cited in the
manuscript. Intended as an educational decision-support synthesis, not a clinical
practice guideline.
